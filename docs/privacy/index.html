Ballistic Escalation: privacy policy

Last updated: 26 November 2026

Ballistic Escalation (“the game”) is made by Anna Kontorovich (“we”). This policy explains what information the game and the services built into it use, and the choices you have.

In short:

  • There is no account and no sign-in, and we never ask for your name or email.
  • The game shows ads from Google. Remove Ads turns off the ads between games; with Premium there are no ads at all.
  • It uses RevenueCat to handle its optional purchases: Premium, Remove Ads, maps and coin packs.

What the game keeps on your device

  • What it keeps: your settings, progress, coins (earned and bought), best raids, the last map and level you played, and what you have bought (so it works offline). They stay on your device. We cannot see them, and the game does not send them to us.
  • Reset progress (in Settings) clears your progress and the coins you earned. Coins you bought, and what you bought with them, are kept.
  • To delete everything: clear the app’s data or uninstall the game. Restore purchases can then bring back Premium, Remove Ads and maps, but not coins: coins live only in the game’s data on your device.
  • Backups: on Android, the game’s data is included in your own Android backup (if it is on), so your progress and coins can move to a new phone. On iPhone and iPad, your device’s backup may include it. These backups are controlled by your device settings; we cannot see them.

Ads (Google AdMob)

  • If you own Premium, the game shows no ads. Its rewards come from a Claim button instead.
  • If you bought Remove Ads, there are no ads between games. Ads you choose to watch for a reward are still offered, and always optional.
  • Otherwise: ads you choose to watch for a reward, and an ad on the end screen after some finished games. There are never ads during a raid.
  • What Google’s ad software collects and shares (to show and measure ads and to prevent fraud):
    • your IP address, which gives an approximate location;
    • your device’s advertising ID and app set ID;
    • how you use the app and the ads (for example app launches, taps and ad views);
    • diagnostics (for example crash and performance data).
    It is sent encrypted.
  • Consent: where the law requires it (for example in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, and in some US states), Google’s consent screen asks you first, and “Privacy choices” in Settings lets you change your choice at any time.
  • The consent screen itself uses your approximate location, how you interact with it, and performance data, to show the right form and remember your choice.
  • iPhone and iPad: Apple’s tracking prompt asks whether the advertising ID may be used to track you. If you say no, it is not used for tracking, and the game works exactly the same.
  • Android: you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your device’s settings.
  • Fewer personal ads: saying no means the ads may be less personal, but ads still appear (unless you own Premium or Remove Ads).
  • Google’s own rules: how Google uses this data is in Google’s privacy policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy) and on its page “How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services” (https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites).

Purchases (through RevenueCat)

  • What you can buy: Premium, Remove Ads and each map, bought once; and coin packs, which can be bought again. All are optional and paid through the App Store or Google Play. We never see your card or payment details.
  • What RevenueCat receives: RevenueCat, the service we use to handle purchases, receives an anonymous ID it creates for this game and your purchase history, meaning what was bought and when, with the store’s receipt. It uses them to unlock your purchases and to restore them. It receives no name, email or location, and the data is sent encrypted.
  • Restore purchases (in Settings and in the game’s Store) brings back Premium, Remove Ads and maps. Coins are not restored, because there are no accounts: they are kept only with your game’s data (see above).
  • RevenueCat’s privacy policy: https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy.
  • Deleting purchase records: to ask for your purchase records to be deleted, contact us (below).

Internet and permissions

  • Internet: the game uses the internet only for ads and purchases. The game itself plays without a connection.
  • Android permissions: internet and network state, vibration (you can turn it off in Settings), the advertising ID, Android’s ad services (ad topics and ad measurement, which you can turn off in Android’s settings), background work for Google’s ads library (a wake lock and a foreground service), and Google Play billing. None of these shows a prompt.
  • iPhone and iPad: the only system permission prompt is Apple’s tracking prompt. Google’s consent screen can also appear (see Consent above).
  • Never used: location, camera, microphone, contacts, photos and files.

Children

  • The game is meant for ages 13 and over and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect information from children under 13.

How long data is kept

  • On your device: until you reset progress, clear the app’s data or uninstall.
  • Google: under Google’s own policies (above).
  • RevenueCat: as long as it is needed to provide and restore your purchases, or until you ask us to delete them.

Changes to this policy

  • If the game’s use of data changes, we will update this policy and its date before that version is released. The game will ask again if your consent is needed.
